🗂️ ASSET Asset, Change, and Configuration Management

Manage the organization's IT and OT assets — including hardware, software, and data — and the changes and configurations applied to them. Knowing what you have and controlling how it changes is fundamental to all other cybersecurity functions.

MIL 1 — Performed
  • ASSET-1a Establish and maintain an inventory of IT assets
  • ASSET-1b Establish and maintain an inventory of OT assets
  • ASSET-2a Perform change management for organizational assets
  • ASSET-2b Test changes to assets before deployment
  • ASSET-3a Establish baseline configurations for IT and OT assets
  • ASSET-3b Manage configurations for IT and OT assets
MIL 2 — Managed
  • ASSET-1c Prioritize assets based on criticality to operations
  • ASSET-1d Ensure asset inventories are kept current
  • ASSET-2c Plan and document change management activities
  • ASSET-3c Define and use documented configuration management procedures
MIL 3 — Optimized
  • ASSET-1e Analyze asset management activities to identify improvements
  • ASSET-1f Communicate asset management results to stakeholders
  • ASSET-2d Improve change management based on lessons learned
  • ASSET-3d Review and improve the configuration management program
See Example:
MIL 1 — PerformedAsset Management — Basic Inventory
The IT team at a regional water utility uses a shared spreadsheet to manually list all servers, workstations, and network switches. The OT team maintains a separate paper log of PLCs and HMIs at pump stations. When a new laptop is purchased, a technician adds it to the spreadsheet. No formal process exists for removing decommissioned assets. Real-World Scenario
A network administrator installs a new firewall and adds "Firewall-HQ-01" to the Excel inventory with its IP address, make, and model. The PLC controlling a chemical dosing pump is cataloged on a whiteboard in the control room. Both inventories exist — but are siloed, inconsistent, and manually updated.
MIL 2 — ManagedAsset Management — Documented & Prioritized
The utility deploys an automated asset discovery tool (e.g., Nessus, Lansweeper) that continuously scans the network and populates a centralized CMDB. Assets are tagged by criticality tier: Tier 1 (mission-critical), Tier 2 (important), Tier 3 (non-critical). A formal Change Advisory Board (CAB) reviews all proposed changes to Tier 1 assets before approval. Configuration baselines are documented in a Standard Operating Procedure. Real-World Scenario
Before upgrading the SCADA historian server (Tier 1), the change request is submitted to the CAB with rollback procedures. The CMDB automatically flags that the server has 3 dependent devices. The change is scheduled during a maintenance window, tested in a staging environment first, and the CMDB is auto-updated upon deployment.
MIL 3 — OptimizedAsset Management — Continuous Improvement
The utility's CISO presents a quarterly Asset Management scorecard to the executive team, showing inventory completeness rates, mean-time-to-update after change, and configuration drift metrics. A post-incident review revealed that an undiscovered IoT sensor created a blind spot — leading to a new policy requiring passive OT discovery scans. Lessons learned are formally incorporated into the Configuration Management Program annually. Real-World Scenario
After a penetration test discovers an unmanaged network tap, the CISO leads a root-cause analysis. The finding is escalated to the board, a new asset onboarding checklist is mandated, and the discovery tool's scan frequency is increased for OT segments. The improvement closes the gap and is documented in the next C2M2 self-evaluation cycle.
🛡️ THREAT Threat and Vulnerability Management

Identify, analyze, and respond to cybersecurity threats and vulnerabilities affecting the organization's assets and operations. Includes vulnerability scanning, threat intelligence, and remediation tracking.

MIL 1 — Performed
  • THREAT-1a Identify cybersecurity vulnerabilities in organizational assets
  • THREAT-1b Evaluate cybersecurity vulnerabilities to determine risk
  • THREAT-1c Respond to discovered cybersecurity vulnerabilities
  • THREAT-2a Perform threat analysis activities
  • THREAT-2b Receive cybersecurity threat intelligence from external sources
MIL 2 — Managed
  • THREAT-1d Plan and document vulnerability management activities
  • THREAT-1e Track remediation of identified vulnerabilities
  • THREAT-2c Document and manage threat analysis activities
  • THREAT-2d Prioritize threats based on operational impact
MIL 3 — Optimized
  • THREAT-1f Analyze vulnerability management activities to improve the program
  • THREAT-2e Analyze threat data to improve threat management practices
  • THREAT-2f Share threat intelligence across organizational boundaries
See Example:
MIL 1 — PerformedThreat & Vulnerability — Basic Scanning
A hospital IT team runs a monthly Nessus vulnerability scan against internet-facing servers. When a critical CVE is flagged, the sysadmin patches it — usually within a few weeks. The organization subscribes to US-CERT email alerts for threat intelligence. Remediation is handled informally with no tracking system. Real-World Scenario
A scan reveals CVE-2023-XXXX affecting the hospital's VPN gateway. The sysadmin downloads the vendor patch, applies it after hours, and emails the IT director to confirm. There is no ticket, no SLA, and no verification that the patch was successful — but the vulnerability is no longer detected in the next scan.
MIL 2 — ManagedThreat & Vulnerability — Tracked Remediation
The hospital adopts a Vulnerability Management Policy with defined SLAs: Critical CVEs patched within 7 days, High within 30, Medium within 90. All vulnerabilities are tracked in a ticketing system (ServiceNow). Threat intelligence feeds (ISAC, CISA KEV catalog) are reviewed weekly by the security team. High-impact threats to EHR and medical devices are prioritized over general IT. Real-World Scenario
A CISA alert warns of active exploitation of a flaw in a common medical imaging platform. The security analyst cross-references the CMDB, finds 4 affected PACS servers, opens remediation tickets with a 7-day SLA, and notifies the biomedical engineering team. A weekly dashboard tracks open critical vulnerabilities to closure.
MIL 3 — OptimizedThreat & Vulnerability — Intelligence Sharing & Improvement
The CISO reviews quarterly metrics on vulnerability mean-time-to-remediate, SLA breach rates, and recurring vulnerability categories. Findings show that 40% of critical vulnerabilities involve unpatched medical devices — driving a new medical device security policy. The hospital actively participates in H-ISAC, contributing anonymized threat data and receiving early warnings of healthcare-targeted campaigns. Real-World Scenario
Analysis of 12-month vulnerability data reveals that the radiology network consistently has the longest remediation times. The CISO presents this trend to the CFO, secures budget for a dedicated biomedical security engineer, and establishes a shared threat-intelligence feed with two peer hospital systems in the region.
⚖️ RISK Risk Management

Establish and maintain a cybersecurity risk management strategy, assess cybersecurity risks, and communicate risk information to support organizational decision-making.

MIL 1 — Performed
  • RISK-1a Identify cybersecurity risks to the organization
  • RISK-1b Analyze identified cybersecurity risks
  • RISK-2a Respond to identified cybersecurity risks
  • RISK-2b Prioritize risks for response based on impact
MIL 2 — Managed
  • RISK-1c Establish and document a risk management strategy
  • RISK-1d Define risk tolerance and acceptance criteria
  • RISK-2c Track and document risk responses
  • RISK-2d Align risk management with organizational objectives
MIL 3 — Optimized
  • RISK-1e Review and improve the risk management program
  • RISK-2e Communicate cybersecurity risk to executive leadership
  • RISK-2f Integrate cybersecurity risk into enterprise risk management
See Example:
MIL 1 — PerformedRisk Management — Ad-Hoc Identification
A municipal government's IT team informally identifies risks after incidents or audits. When a ransomware attack hits a neighboring city, the IT director makes a list of potential risks (no backups, open RDP ports, no MFA) and addresses the most obvious ones first with available budget. No formal risk register or scoring methodology exists. Real-World Scenario
Following a phishing email that nearly compromised the tax system, the IT director identifies "lack of email filtering" as a high risk and purchases a spam filter. The decision is reactive, undocumented, and driven by the incident — not a structured risk analysis process.
MIL 2 — ManagedRisk Management — Documented Strategy & Risk Register
The city adopts a formal Cybersecurity Risk Management Strategy. A risk register is maintained with each identified risk scored by likelihood and impact (5×5 matrix). Risk tolerance thresholds are defined: risks scoring above 15 require immediate mitigation; 10–14 require a remediation plan within 90 days; below 10 are accepted with documented rationale. All risk responses are tracked in the register. Real-World Scenario
The risk register identifies "unpatched legacy systems in the permitting database" with a score of 18 (high likelihood × high impact). A mitigation plan is approved: migrate to a cloud-hosted platform within 6 months. Monthly status updates are logged in the risk register until closure.
MIL 3 — OptimizedRisk Management — Enterprise Integration & Leadership Reporting
The CISO presents a quarterly cybersecurity risk dashboard to the City Council, translating technical risks into financial exposure estimates (e.g., "ransomware risk carries an estimated $2.3M impact potential"). Cybersecurity risk is formally integrated into the city's Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) framework, reported alongside financial and operational risks. Annual risk program reviews incorporate threat landscape changes. Real-World Scenario
When the city considers a new smart-parking IoT deployment, the CISO performs a pre-deployment risk assessment and presents the findings to the City Manager. The assessment identifies supply chain risks in the IoT vendor, leading to contractual security requirements before the contract is signed — preventing risk rather than reacting to it.
🔑 ACCESS Identity and Access Management

Manage user identities and control access to organizational assets. Ensure that only authorized individuals can access systems and data, and that access is appropriate for their role.

MIL 1 — Performed
  • ACCESS-1a Establish and maintain user identities
  • ACCESS-1b Authenticate users before granting access
  • ACCESS-2a Control access to organizational assets based on need
  • ACCESS-2b Remove access when no longer required
  • ACCESS-3a Enforce least-privilege access controls
MIL 2 — Managed
  • ACCESS-1c Document and manage the identity lifecycle
  • ACCESS-2c Implement multi-factor authentication for privileged accounts
  • ACCESS-3b Review and audit access rights periodically
  • ACCESS-3c Manage privileged access separately from standard access
MIL 3 — Optimized
  • ACCESS-1d Improve identity management based on review findings
  • ACCESS-3d Analyze access management results and communicate to leadership
  • ACCESS-3e Continuously improve access management practices
See Example:
MIL 1 — PerformedIdentity & Access — Basic Accounts
An electric cooperative creates user accounts for all employees in Active Directory and requires username/password to log in. IT restricts access to the billing system to billing staff. When an employee leaves, HR notifies IT verbally to disable the account — sometimes days later. No MFA is deployed; no formal identity lifecycle process exists. Real-World Scenario
A billing clerk is promoted to supervisor. Her IT access is updated manually by the helpdesk — but her old billing-only permissions are never removed, leaving her with more access than her new role requires. The over-provisioning goes undetected because no access reviews are performed.
MIL 2 — ManagedIdentity & Access — MFA, PAM, and Lifecycle
The cooperative deploys a Privileged Access Management (PAM) solution. All admin accounts require MFA via hardware tokens. A formal identity lifecycle SOP governs onboarding (role-based access provisioning), transfers (access review), and offboarding (same-day account disable within 4 hours of HR notification). Quarterly access reviews are conducted by department managers. Real-World Scenario
During a quarterly access review, a manager discovers a former contractor's account still has read access to the SCADA historian. The account is immediately disabled, the incident is documented, and the onboarding/offboarding SOP is updated to include contractor accounts in the automated HR-IT workflow.
MIL 3 — OptimizedIdentity & Access — Continuous Improvement & Zero Trust
The cooperative's security team analyzes access review findings quarterly and identifies that 12% of accounts have excessive privileges. This drives a Zero Trust segmentation project, implementing Just-in-Time (JIT) access for SCADA systems. The CISO reports access posture metrics — including privileged account counts and MFA adoption rates — to the board annually. Real-World Scenario
Analysis reveals that third-party vendors frequently retain 24/7 access to OT systems even during non-maintenance periods. JIT access controls are deployed: vendors now request a time-limited session through the PAM portal, approved by an OT engineer. Standing vendor access to critical systems is eliminated entirely.
👁️ SITUATION Situational Awareness

Collect, analyze, and communicate cybersecurity information to develop an accurate picture of the organization's cybersecurity posture. Includes logging, monitoring, and sharing of security-relevant information.

MIL 1 — Performed
  • SITUATION-1a Perform logging on IT assets
  • SITUATION-1b Perform logging on OT assets
  • SITUATION-2a Analyze log data to identify cybersecurity events
  • SITUATION-2b Receive and review cybersecurity information from external sources
MIL 2 — Managed
  • SITUATION-1c Define and document logging requirements
  • SITUATION-1d Protect log data from unauthorized modification
  • SITUATION-2c Document and manage situational awareness activities
  • SITUATION-2d Share cybersecurity situational awareness information internally
MIL 3 — Optimized
  • SITUATION-1e Review and improve the logging program
  • SITUATION-2e Analyze situational awareness activities for improvements
  • SITUATION-2f Share situational awareness information with sector peers
See Example:
MIL 1 — PerformedSituational Awareness — Basic Logging
A natural gas pipeline operator enables Windows Event Logging on servers and firewall logging on the perimeter firewall. Logs are stored locally on each device and reviewed manually when something seems wrong. The operations team subscribes to ICS-CERT email alerts but reviews them inconsistently. Real-World Scenario
After a suspicious login alert from the IT manager, the sysadmin manually reviews the Windows Security Event Log on the authentication server. The log shows 200 failed login attempts from an external IP — but the log was never forwarded to a central location, and by the time it's reviewed, the logs have partially overwritten due to limited local storage.
MIL 2 — ManagedSituational Awareness — SIEM with Documented Requirements
The operator deploys a SIEM (Security Information and Event Management) system that centralizes logs from firewalls, Windows/Linux servers, VPN, and SCADA historian. A Logging Requirements Policy defines what must be logged, retention periods (minimum 1 year), and log integrity protections (write-once storage). SIEM correlation rules auto-alert analysts to brute-force attempts, off-hours logins, and SCADA command anomalies. Real-World Scenario
The SIEM correlates an off-hours VPN login from an engineer's account with a subsequent connection to the SCADA HMI — unusual for that user. An automated alert is sent to the on-call security analyst, who calls the engineer and confirms it was unauthorized access. The incident is contained within 20 minutes because of centralized, real-time log correlation.
MIL 3 — OptimizedSituational Awareness — Sector Intelligence Sharing
The operator's security team analyzes 6-month SIEM data and discovers that 80% of alerts are false positives from a single misconfigured OT device, reducing analyst efficiency. SIEM rules are tuned accordingly. The company joins the ONG-ISAC and contributes anonymized Indicators of Compromise (IoCs) from their incident, receiving early warning of a threat actor targeting pipeline SCADA systems — which they immediately use to update detection rules. Real-World Scenario
An ONG-ISAC advisory describes a new spearphishing campaign targeting pipeline control engineers. Within 4 hours, the security team creates SIEM detection rules for the described IoCs, briefs the operations team, and emails targeted engineers with awareness guidance — all before the campaign reaches their network.
🚨 RESPONSE Event and Incident Response, Continuity of Operations

Detect, escalate, respond to, and recover from cybersecurity events and incidents. Ensure that critical operations can continue through and after a cybersecurity incident.

MIL 1 — Performed
  • RESPONSE-1a Detect cybersecurity events
  • RESPONSE-2a Escalate and report cybersecurity events
  • RESPONSE-3a Respond to declared cybersecurity incidents
  • RESPONSE-3b Contain cybersecurity incidents
  • RESPONSE-4a Identify functions that must continue during an incident
  • RESPONSE-4b Back up information needed to sustain operations
MIL 2 — Managed
  • RESPONSE-1b Document and plan detection activities
  • RESPONSE-2b Document escalation and reporting procedures
  • RESPONSE-3c Document and manage the incident response plan
  • RESPONSE-3d Train incident response personnel
  • RESPONSE-4c Document and maintain continuity of operations plans
  • RESPONSE-4d Test continuity and backup procedures
MIL 3 — Optimized
  • RESPONSE-3e Conduct post-incident reviews and incorporate lessons learned
  • RESPONSE-3f Communicate incident response capabilities to stakeholders
  • RESPONSE-4e Improve continuity plans based on exercise results
See Example:
MIL 1 — PerformedIncident Response — Reactive Detection & Containment
A manufacturing plant detects ransomware when employees report they cannot open files. The IT manager disconnects affected workstations from the network (containment) and calls in outside help. Backups exist but have not been tested. The plant floor continues operating manually while IT systems are rebuilt over 5 days. No formal IRP exists. Real-World Scenario
At 7 AM, production floor staff notice workstations displaying ransom notes. The IT manager physically unplugs switches to isolate the network. The plant shifts to paper-based production tracking. After 5 days, systems are restored from backup tapes — but two backups are corrupted, and recovery is incomplete.
MIL 2 — ManagedIncident Response — Documented IRP & Trained Team
The plant develops a formal Incident Response Plan with defined roles (IR Lead, Legal, Communications, IT, OT), escalation thresholds, and notification timelines (notify CEO within 2 hours of a confirmed incident). The IR team conducts a tabletop exercise annually. Backup restoration procedures are tested quarterly. A Business Continuity Plan identifies that production scheduling is the first system to restore. Real-World Scenario
When malware is detected on the ERP system, the IR Lead activates the IRP within 15 minutes. Legal is notified (for insurance), IT isolates the affected segment via pre-configured VLAN rules, and OT systems (on a separate network) continue uninterrupted. Recovery of the ERP from a tested backup takes 6 hours — not 5 days.
MIL 3 — OptimizedIncident Response — Lessons Learned & Continuous Improvement
After each exercise and real incident, the plant holds a formal after-action review. Findings are formally incorporated into IRP updates. The CISO reports IR readiness metrics (mean-time-to-detect, mean-time-to-contain, exercise results) to the board annually. The continuity plan is improved after a tabletop reveals that the manual production fallback takes 4 hours to activate — driving a project to pre-stage printed runbooks at each workstation. Real-World Scenario
A red team exercise reveals that the IR team has no process for communicating with customers during an outage. This gap is escalated to the CEO. A customer-communication protocol is added to the IRP, a draft notification template is prepared, and the communications director is added to the IR team roster — all documented before the next exercise.
🤝 THIRD-PARTIES Third-Party Risk Management

Identify and manage cybersecurity risks introduced by third-party relationships, including vendors, suppliers, service providers, and partners who have access to or impact organizational systems.

MIL 1 — Performed
  • THIRD-1a Identify third parties with access to organizational assets
  • THIRD-2a Establish cybersecurity requirements for third parties
  • THIRD-3a Address cybersecurity risks from third parties
  • THIRD-4a Include cybersecurity requirements in third-party agreements
MIL 2 — Managed
  • THIRD-1b Prioritize third parties by risk level
  • THIRD-2b Monitor third parties for compliance with cybersecurity requirements
  • THIRD-3b Document and track third-party risk activities
  • THIRD-4b Review and update third-party agreements periodically
MIL 3 — Optimized
  • THIRD-1c Analyze third-party risk data to improve the program
  • THIRD-3c Communicate third-party risk to leadership
  • THIRD-4c Continuously improve third-party risk management practices
See Example:
MIL 1 — PerformedThird-Party Risk — Basic Vendor Identification
A financial services firm maintains a list of major IT vendors (cloud provider, payroll processor, IT support contractor). Contracts include a general data protection clause but no specific cybersecurity requirements. Vendor security is assumed, not verified. When the IT contractor needs remote access, they are given a VPN account with no time limits. Real-World Scenario
The firm's cloud HR platform vendor suffers a breach that exposes employee PII. The firm learns about it from a news article — not from the vendor. There is no breach-notification clause in the contract, no SLA for notification, and no process to assess the impact. The response is entirely reactive.
MIL 2 — ManagedThird-Party Risk — Tiered Vendors & Ongoing Monitoring
The firm categorizes vendors into Tier 1 (critical, direct access to sensitive data), Tier 2 (important, indirect access), and Tier 3 (low risk). Tier 1 vendors must provide annual SOC 2 Type II reports and complete a security questionnaire. Contracts are updated to include breach notification within 72 hours. A third-party risk register tracks all vendors, risk scores, and review dates. Real-World Scenario
Annual review of the payment processor's SOC 2 report reveals a control gap in their access management. The firm issues a formal remediation request with a 60-day deadline. The vendor's response and remediation status are tracked in the third-party risk register. The contract renewal is conditioned on closure of the gap.
MIL 3 — OptimizedThird-Party Risk — Program Improvement & Board Reporting
The CISO presents a quarterly third-party risk summary to the board, highlighting highest-risk vendors and concentration risk (e.g., "35% of critical functions rely on a single cloud provider"). Analysis of vendor incident data over 2 years reveals that smaller niche vendors have higher breach rates — leading to a new minimum-security requirement for all vendors regardless of tier. Real-World Scenario
A supply chain analysis reveals the firm's core banking platform vendor uses a sub-processor in a geopolitically sensitive region. The CISO escalates this to the board as a concentration risk. A contingency plan is developed for a backup core banking option, and the primary vendor is required to disclose all sub-processors annually as a new contract condition.
👷 WORKFORCE Workforce Management

Establish and maintain the cybersecurity knowledge, skills, and abilities of the workforce. Manage personnel practices in a cybersecurity context, including hiring, training, and departure.

MIL 1 — Performed
  • WORK-1a Assign cybersecurity responsibilities to staff
  • WORK-2a Provide cybersecurity awareness training to all personnel
  • WORK-2b Provide role-based cybersecurity training
  • WORK-3a Screen personnel for cybersecurity risk
MIL 2 — Managed
  • WORK-1b Document cybersecurity roles and responsibilities
  • WORK-2c Plan and track training completion
  • WORK-2d Verify staff have skills to perform assigned duties
  • WORK-3b Manage personnel practices (onboarding/offboarding) with cybersecurity in mind
MIL 3 — Optimized
  • WORK-1c Review and improve the cybersecurity workforce program
  • WORK-2e Analyze training effectiveness and improve program
  • WORK-3c Communicate workforce cybersecurity posture to leadership
See Example:
MIL 1 — PerformedWorkforce — Basic Awareness Training
A government agency assigns cybersecurity responsibility to the IT Director. All new employees complete a one-time online security awareness module during onboarding. IT staff attend an annual CompTIA Security+ refresher. Background checks are run on all new hires. No tracking of training completion exists beyond HR's onboarding checklist. Real-World Scenario
A new data entry clerk completes the onboarding security module on day one. 18 months later, she falls for a phishing email because she has received no follow-up training and was never tested via simulated phishing. The agency has no mechanism to detect this skills gap before an incident occurs.
MIL 2 — ManagedWorkforce — Tracked Training & Role-Based Skills Verification
The agency's Learning Management System (LMS) tracks cybersecurity training completion for all employees. Annual phishing simulation results are reviewed — departments with high click rates receive additional targeted training. Cybersecurity roles are documented in job descriptions with required certifications. System administrators must hold Security+ or equivalent; the CISO must hold CISSP. New hires cannot be granted system access until training is completed. Real-World Scenario
The LMS shows that the Public Works department has the lowest phishing simulation pass rate (42%) of any department. The security team delivers a targeted 30-minute phishing awareness session to Public Works staff, and run rate improves to 81% in the next simulated test. Results are reported to the department head monthly until the target (90%) is met.
MIL 3 — OptimizedWorkforce — Program Analysis & Leadership Reporting
The CISO presents an annual Workforce Cybersecurity Posture report to the agency director, including training completion rates, phishing simulation trends, certification currency, and skills gap analysis. Analysis of incidents over 3 years shows 60% involved human error — driving investment in a behavior-based security culture program rather than just compliance training. Offboarding now includes a structured credential revocation checklist verified within 2 hours. Real-World Scenario
The workforce analysis reveals a critical skills gap: only 2 of 12 IT staff have hands-on OT/ICS security training. The CISO secures budget for 4 staff to attend an ICS security course, establishes a cross-training rotation with the OT team, and sets a 2-year goal for all senior IT staff to earn GICSP certification. Progress is reported quarterly to the director.
🏗️ ARCHITECTURE Cybersecurity Architecture

Define and implement a cybersecurity architecture strategy that guides how the organization protects its systems and data through network design, asset hardening, and secure software development practices.

MIL 1 — Performed
  • ARCH-1a Define a cybersecurity architecture for the organization
  • ARCH-2a Implement network protections (e.g., firewalls, segmentation)
  • ARCH-3a Harden IT and OT assets (disable unnecessary services)
  • ARCH-4a Implement secure software development practices
MIL 2 — Managed
  • ARCH-1b Document and maintain the cybersecurity architecture
  • ARCH-2b Manage and document network protection controls
  • ARCH-3b Define and enforce asset hardening standards
  • ARCH-4b Evaluate software acquisitions for cybersecurity risk
MIL 3 — Optimized
  • ARCH-1c Review and improve the cybersecurity architecture program
  • ARCH-2c Analyze network protection effectiveness
  • ARCH-3c Communicate architecture posture to leadership
See Example:
MIL 1 — PerformedArchitecture — Basic Network Protections
A research institution installs a perimeter firewall, enables HTTPS on all web servers, and disables Telnet in favor of SSH on network devices. A high-level network diagram exists but is outdated. CIS Benchmark hardening is applied to new servers but not consistently enforced on existing systems. No formal architecture documentation exists. Real-World Scenario
The university's IT team installs a next-gen firewall at the campus perimeter and blocks inbound traffic to known malicious IPs. However, the research lab network is not segmented from the administrative network — a compromise of a researcher's workstation could allow lateral movement to HR and financial systems.
MIL 2 — ManagedArchitecture — Documented & Enforced Standards
The university documents a formal network architecture that segments research, administrative, student, and guest WiFi into separate VLANs with controlled inter-segment routing. A Hardening Standard (based on CIS Benchmarks) is applied to all new systems before deployment and validated via automated compliance scanning. All software purchases must pass a security evaluation checklist before procurement approval. Real-World Scenario
A department wants to deploy a new cloud-based collaboration tool. Before purchase, IT Security reviews the vendor's SOC 2 report, checks data residency, and verifies API security controls. One tool is rejected because it lacks MFA support. The approved tool is onboarded through a documented secure configuration process.
MIL 3 — OptimizedArchitecture — Effectiveness Analysis & Strategic Reporting
The CISO reviews quarterly metrics on firewall rule utilization, network segmentation violations, and hardening compliance scores. Analysis reveals that 30% of firewall rules are unused — a finding that triggers a firewall rule cleanup project reducing the attack surface. Architecture posture (segmentation maturity, encryption coverage, hardening compliance) is reported to the board as part of the annual security program review. Real-World Scenario
A network effectiveness analysis reveals that the research network and the regulated CUI (Controlled Unclassified Information) network share a common DNS server — a segmentation gap that could enable data exfiltration. The gap is escalated to the CISO, a dedicated CUI DNS server is deployed within 30 days, and the architecture diagram is updated and approved by the board.
📋 PROGRAM Cybersecurity Program Management

Establish and maintain a cybersecurity program that integrates all security activities across the organization. Provide executive leadership and governance to support a consistent, organization-wide security posture.

MIL 1 — Performed
  • PROGRAM-1a Establish a cybersecurity program
  • PROGRAM-1b Define the scope of the cybersecurity program
  • PROGRAM-2a Assign authority and accountability for cybersecurity
  • PROGRAM-3a Coordinate cybersecurity requirements across the organization
MIL 2 — Managed
  • PROGRAM-1c Document cybersecurity program strategy and objectives
  • PROGRAM-2b Fund and resource the cybersecurity program
  • PROGRAM-2c Track cybersecurity program performance
  • PROGRAM-3b Align the cybersecurity program with organizational risk management
MIL 3 — Optimized
  • PROGRAM-1d Review and improve the cybersecurity program strategy
  • PROGRAM-2d Communicate cybersecurity program performance to executive leadership
  • PROGRAM-3c Integrate cybersecurity into enterprise governance processes
See Example:
MIL 1 — PerformedProgram Management — Informal Cybersecurity Program
A regional bank designates the IT Manager as the person responsible for cybersecurity. An Acceptable Use Policy exists. IT makes security decisions as needed with available budget. There is no written cybersecurity strategy, no defined program scope, and no mechanism for measuring program performance. Cybersecurity decisions are reactive and technology-driven rather than risk-driven. Real-World Scenario
The bank purchases a new EDR tool after a salesperson's pitch. There is no formal process for evaluating whether this investment addresses the bank's highest risks — or whether the budget would be better spent elsewhere. The CISO role doesn't exist; the IT Manager reports to the COO and handles security between other responsibilities.
MIL 2 — ManagedProgram Management — Strategy, Budget, and Performance Tracking
The bank hires a CISO and publishes a 3-year Cybersecurity Strategic Plan aligned to NIST CSF. An annual cybersecurity budget is formally approved by the board. KPIs are tracked: patch compliance rate, mean-time-to-detect, training completion %, and open high-risk items. Cybersecurity requirements are coordinated across all departments via a quarterly Security Steering Committee. Real-World Scenario
The CISO presents the Q2 cybersecurity scorecard to the Security Steering Committee: patch compliance is at 94% (target 98%), phishing click rate improved to 6% (from 14% last year), and 3 of 5 high-priority risk items are closed. The remaining 2 items have updated remediation dates and resource plans documented.
MIL 3 — OptimizedProgram Management — Enterprise Governance Integration
Cybersecurity is formally embedded in the bank's enterprise governance. The CISO presents annually to the full Board of Directors. Cybersecurity risk is a standing agenda item at the Board Risk Committee. The strategic plan is reviewed annually and updated based on the evolving threat landscape, regulatory changes, and program performance data. Cybersecurity is integrated into new product development, M&A due diligence, and capital expenditure processes. Real-World Scenario
When the bank considers acquiring a fintech startup, the CISO is included in the due diligence team. A cybersecurity assessment reveals the startup has no patch management process and stores customer PII in an unencrypted database. This finding is factored into the acquisition price negotiation, and a mandatory 90-day remediation plan is written into the acquisition agreement.
